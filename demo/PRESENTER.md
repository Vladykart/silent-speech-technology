# Presenter script (3–5 minutes)

## 0:00–0:35 — Start with the truth

> “Before I touch anything: this is a concept and simulated pipeline. Every trace, feature, candidate, score, threshold, delay, and failure is authored. There is no wearable, model, microphone, camera, biometric input, or project accuracy result behind this page. We built it to test the interaction and its failure behavior.”

Point to the green simulation badge and the “what you are not seeing” row. **Do not silently mouth a phrase**; that would imply the browser sensed you.

## 0:35–0:55 — Unlock deliberately

Point to **LOCKED**, then choose **Unlock concept**.

> “The proposed decoder is explicitly locked until the wearer chooses to use it. Here that is page state—not implemented device security. Contact and deliberate unlock are design requirements.”

Point to the illustrative 12-command grammar.

> “This grammar is product copy, not a validated vocabulary. The first commercial study is for e& frontline retail advisors and field technicians, with low-consequence lookup and workflow commands.”

## 0:55–1:45 — Walk a clear path

With **Clear command** selected, press **Run next stage**.

> “A future research system would begin with consented surface-EMG channels. These normalized traces are authored. Stage two would check signal quality, segment an attempted command, and form features.”

Advance to candidates.

> “A trustworthy decoder exposes alternatives. These candidates and scores are scripted to show the contract the product must eventually satisfy.”

Advance to the gate; select **Confirm candidate**.

> “Clearing a threshold still does not authorize output. The person confirms. The privacy log now shows one simulated text event and zero raw-fixture egress. No network request occurred.”

## 1:45–2:50 — Make failure and repair the product

Press `2` for **Ambiguous input**, then Space three times.

> “This case is designed to fail. The top scripted score is `0.54`, below the authored `0.72` gate. No output can pass.”

Choose **Choose authored repair**.

> “The fallback is explicit: choose a clarified command or use another input. The repair is manually authored, not a second model prediction. It remains behind confirmation.”

Choose **Confirm candidate**.

> “Only after repair and confirmation does the simulated text counter move. The project has no measured error distribution; published evidence only tells us why this interaction matters.”

If asked for the closest external result:

> “SilentWear, a 2026 preprint, reports four participants, eight commands plus rest, and average silent-command accuracy of 77.5% in its cross-validated setting and 59.3% on a held-out session. That is their neckband result, not ours.”

## 2:50–3:45 — Privacy and evidence boundary

Point to **Design controls** and the egress log.

> “Proposed defaults are lock state, contact required, raw-signal retention and egress off, and confirmation before action. This demo only visualizes those controls; it does not prove encryption, isolation, deletion, safety, PDPL compliance, or secure hardware.”

> “A real study needs voluntariness, a non-biometric alternative, electrical-safety and ethics review, purpose and retention limits, access control, withdrawal/deletion, incident handling, and separation from employee performance management.”

## 3:45–4:30 — Close with the approved path

> “The beachhead is e& frontline retail advisors and field technicians. The commercial lead path is an AED 1,000,000 fixed-fee 90-day pilot, then per-seat annual deployment only if pre-agreed evidence gates pass. Strategic IP and governed bilingual-dataset funding is upside, not a substitute for a working pilot, and later amounts remain TBD.”

> “The next decision is to name the workflow and governance owners, freeze the protocol and kill criteria, and begin discovery before any biosignal collection.”

Transition to the deck’s evidence, roadmap, and ask slides.

## Never say

- “It reads thoughts,” “works behind the ear,” “the model heard that,” or “live.”
- “84% accurate” or any interpretation of fixture scores as measured performance.
- “Real published latency/error” about the demo. Its timing and failure are authored.
- “Private,” “secure,” “on-device,” “compliant,” or “raw never leaves” about a device that does not exist; say **design target**.
- “For ALS/laryngectomy/aphasia users” as a current claim; that requires participatory clinical research and a separate intended-use path.
- Customer, partner, traction, patent ownership, regional leadership, or guaranteed acquisition/exit.
